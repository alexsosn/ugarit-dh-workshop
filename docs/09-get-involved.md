# Get involved & keep learning


You just ran real analyses on a 3,000-year-old corpus, watched an AI build a
research tool, and saw where it needs a human who knows the texts. None of this
required you to be a programmer — it required curiosity and a willingness to
check. Here is how to keep going.

## Contribute to the corpus

Ugaritic studies is small enough that one careful person makes a visible
difference — and large enough that there is real work to do. Ways in, from
lightest to deepest:

- **Spot and report errors.** Reading a transliteration and something looks off?
  Open an issue on the relevant repo. Corrections are contributions.
- **Help annotate.** CUC currently has no lemma / part-of-speech layer; building and
  checking that is exactly the kind of task where a philologist + AI pair works
  well.
- **Build a small tool.**
- **Extend the resource map.** Found a corpus, image archive, or translation we
  missed? Add it to `docs/00-resources.md`.

## Where to learn Ugaritic

### Courses & programmes
- **Ukrainian Catholic University (UCU)**
- **University of Helsinki**
- **Polis – The Jerusalem Institute of Languages and Humanities** — Ugaritic:
  <https://www.polisjerusalem.org/language/ugaritic/>

### Self-study — textbooks
- **John Huehnergard, *An Introduction to Ugaritic*** — the standard teaching
  grammar.
- **Pierre Bordreuil & Dennis Pardee, *A Manual of Ugaritic*** — grammar + texts + glossary.
